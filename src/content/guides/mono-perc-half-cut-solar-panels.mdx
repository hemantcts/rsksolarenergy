## Mono: the silicon

A monocrystalline cell is cut from a single, continuous crystal of silicon, which gives the electrons a clean path and makes the cell more efficient than a polycrystalline one, made from many small crystals melted together. Polycrystalline panels have largely left the market; almost every panel quoted for a rooftop today is mono.

## PERC: the layer on the back

PERC stands for Passivated Emitter and Rear Cell. A reflective layer on the back of the cell bounces light that went straight through back into the silicon, where some of it is turned into power. For years this was the step that made a good panel.

On UTL's own panels, mono PERC lists between 19.52% (the [390 W](/products/mono-solar-panel-390-watt/)) and 20.10% (the [240 W](/products/mono-solar-panel-240-watt-pack-of-2/)).

## Half-cut: how the cells are wired

Half-cut says nothing about the type of cell. Each cell has been cut into two, and the panel is wired as two halves side by side, which improves two things.

- Each half-cell carries half the current, and power lost as heat in the wiring falls sharply as current falls.
- The two halves of the panel work independently, so a shadow from a water tank or a parapet across one half leaves the other half producing.

Half-cut is now used on most large panels, PERC and TOPCon alike. UTL's [735 W half-cut module](/products/mono-solar-panel-735-watt-half-cut/) is one; its [590 W TOPCon half-cut bifacial](/products/solar-panel-590-watt-topcon-half-cut-bi-facial/) is another.

## Which to choose

For a new home or business rooftop, TOPCon is usually worth the difference. For a small off-grid system or a [solar water pump](/solar-pumps/), where roof space is not the limit, mono PERC does the job for less. Either way, if you want the [PM Surya Ghar subsidy](/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-punjab/), the panels must be DCR; see [DCR and non-DCR panels](/dcr-vs-non-dcr-solar-panels/).
