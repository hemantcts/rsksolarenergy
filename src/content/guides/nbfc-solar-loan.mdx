## What an NBFC is

A non-banking finance company is a lender registered with the Reserve Bank of India that is not a bank. It lends, but cannot offer the savings and current accounts a bank does. Many NBFCs offer personal and consumer loans, and some lend specifically for rooftop solar, sometimes through the installer.

## When an NBFC suits a solar purchase

- The bank has said no, or is taking too long.
- Your income is irregular or hard to document, as it can be for farmers and small traders. NBFCs are often more flexible about that.
- You want the finance arranged at the same time as the quote.

## What it usually costs

NBFCs often charge more than banks, because they take on borrowers a bank would not, or decide faster. So before accepting an NBFC offer, compare it with the cheaper routes:

- the [PM Surya Ghar bank loan](/pm-surya-ghar-solar-loan/), for a home system claiming the subsidy
- a [top-up on your home loan](/home-loan-top-up-for-solar/), if you have one

## What to check before signing

- That the NBFC is registered with the Reserve Bank of India, which publishes the list.
- The rate, and whether it is quoted as a flat rate or on a reducing balance. A flat rate looks lower but costs more.
- The processing fee and any other charges.
- Whether you can repay early, and at what cost.
- The whole agreement, before you sign. Be wary of anyone who wants a fee before the loan is sanctioned.

## The loan and the subsidy

However the system is financed, the [PM Surya Ghar subsidy](/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-punjab/) is paid into your own bank account after the DISCOM inspection. It is not paid to the NBFC and does not reduce the loan.
