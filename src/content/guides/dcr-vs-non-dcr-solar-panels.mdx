## What DCR means

DCR stands for Domestic Content Requirement. A DCR panel uses solar cells made in India. A non-DCR panel may well be assembled in India, but its cells are imported. Only the cells differ, so DCR tells you nothing about the panel's technology: there are DCR and non-DCR versions of TOPCon, mono PERC and bifacial panels.

## Why it matters for the subsidy

The [PM Surya Ghar subsidy](/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-punjab/) pays only for home systems built with DCR panels of a model on the government's ALMM list. The inspector checks the panel model. A non-DCR panel does not qualify, however good it is.

The subsidy is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third, capped at ₹78,000. It is paid into the applicant's own bank account after the DISCOM inspection.

On those figures, DCR panels for a 3 kW system cost roughly ₹27,000 to ₹33,000 more than non-DCR ones, for the panels alone. The subsidy on the same 3 kW system is ₹78,000. So for a home that qualifies, paying the DCR premium comes out well ahead.

## When non-DCR makes sense

If a system is not claiming the subsidy, the extra cost of DCR buys nothing. That covers businesses, which are outside the home subsidy, and [off-grid systems](/off-grid-solar-systems/), which do not qualify. For those, non-DCR panels are the cheaper way to the same generation.

## How to check what you are being sold

- Ask for the DCR certificate for the exact panel model on your quote.
- Check the model is on the ALMM list, which the subsidy also requires.
- Compare quotes like for like. A cheaper quote on non-DCR panels is not cheaper if it loses the subsidy.
