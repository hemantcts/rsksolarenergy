## What TOPCon means

A solar cell loses some of its energy where the silicon meets the metal lines that carry the current away. A TOPCon cell puts an extremely thin layer of oxide at that point, on the back of an N-type silicon cell, and cuts that loss. The name spells it out: Tunnel Oxide Passivated Contact.

Two things follow for a rooftop. The panel makes more watts from the same area, and it holds its output better in heat. Saatvik, for example, publishes a temperature coefficient of minus 0.30% per degree for its N-type TOPCon against minus 0.36% for P-type panels. On a Punjab roof in June, where panels run far above the air temperature, that difference adds up.

Two to four points of efficiency mean fewer panels for the same system, or a bigger system on the same roof. We plan on about 100 square feet of shade-free roof per kW; on a small or crowded roof, TOPCon is often what makes the size you need fit.

## When mono PERC still makes sense

PERC panels are a mature technology and generate well. For a small off-grid job, a pump, or a budget where the roof has room to spare, a PERC panel can cost less per watt and do the job. See [mono PERC and half-cut panels](/mono-perc-half-cut-solar-panels/).

## TOPCon and the subsidy

TOPCon describes how the cell is built. DCR describes where it was made. For the [PM Surya Ghar subsidy](/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-punjab/) the panels must be DCR and on the ALMM list, TOPCon or otherwise. Read [DCR and non-DCR panels](/dcr-vs-non-dcr-solar-panels/) before comparing quotes.
