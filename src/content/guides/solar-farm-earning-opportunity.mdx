## 1. Your own power plant under PM-KUSUM

Component A of PM-KUSUM, the central government's scheme for farmers, lets individual farmers, groups of farmers, cooperatives, panchayats, farmer producer organisations and water user associations set up solar plants of 500 kW to 2 MW. The DISCOM buys the power at a feed-in tariff set by the state electricity regulator.

A few things decide whether this works:

- The DISCOM has to have room for the power. It notifies how much capacity each substation can take and invites applications, so you cannot simply build and connect.
- The tariff is set in the power purchase agreement. Check what it is and whether it changes over the term before you plan around it.
- A plant of this size is a large investment, usually funded with a bank loan. The return depends on the tariff, the building cost and the loan.

The scheme's timelines have changed. The ministry extended the deadlines for projects already awarded, and a follow-on scheme, often called KUSUM 2.0, has been reported as under consideration. In Punjab, watch the notices from PEDA, the state's nodal agency for the scheme, and from PSPCL for the next call for applications.

## 2. Leasing land to a developer

If you have suitable land but not the money or the appetite for a power plant, the scheme allows a developer or the DISCOM to build the plant on your land. You receive a lease rent agreed between you and the developer. It is the lowest-risk route: you give up the use of the land for the length of the lease and take no part in selling the power.

Before signing, have a lawyer read the lease. Check the length, the rent and any increase over the years, who removes the plant at the end, and what happens if the developer stops paying.

## 3. Solar that cuts your own bills

For most farmers and landowners, the most certain return from solar is the money it saves.

- A solar pump replaces diesel or grid power for irrigation. PM-KUSUM Component B subsidises standalone solar pumps through PEDA; see [the solar pump subsidy for Punjab farmers](/blog/pm-kusum-solar-pump-subsidy-punjab-farmers/) and [solar pumps](/solar-pumps/).
- Under Component C, a grid-connected farm pump can be solarised, with the farmer using the power for irrigation and selling the surplus to the DISCOM.
- A farmhouse, dairy, cold store or shop on the land can run on its own rooftop or ground-mounted system; see [commercial solar in Punjab](/commercial-solar-punjab/) and [off-grid systems](/off-grid-solar-systems/) where there is no reliable grid.

## Be wary of promised returns

Anyone offering a guaranteed income from a solar farm, or asking for money to reserve a place in a scheme, should be checked carefully. Scheme applications go through the DISCOM and PEDA. The official sources are listed below.
