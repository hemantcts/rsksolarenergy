A tubular battery is a lead-acid battery built for deep, repeated use, which is why it suits solar. It holds liquid electrolyte, water and sulphuric acid, and a little of the water is split off every time it charges. Most of the care it needs follows from that.

## Top up with distilled water only

The level in each cell falls as the battery charges. Top it up with distilled or battery water, never tap, RO or mineral water, which leave salts in the cells. Never add acid: the acid stays in the battery and only the water is lost.

Fill to the level mark or float indicator and no higher. An overfilled cell spills acid when the battery charges and corrodes the terminals and the stand.

## Keep the terminals clean and tight

White or green crust on the terminals is corrosion, and it resists the current like a partly closed tap. With the system switched off, clean it away, dry the terminals, and coat them lightly with petroleum jelly. A loose terminal heats up under load, so check the connections are tight.

## Give it air and keep it cool

Charging releases a small amount of hydrogen, so a tubular battery belongs in a ventilated place, well away from sparks and open flames. Heat is its other enemy: a battery in a hot, closed room or in direct sun ages faster. A shaded, airy spot on a stand, off a damp floor, is ideal.

## Do not run it flat every day

How far a battery is discharged each day decides how long it lasts. When we size a system, we plan on using only half of a tubular battery's capacity in a normal cycle, which is what lets it last. If your battery is regularly running down to the inverter's cut-off, the battery bank is too small for what you run, and our [hybrid solar calculator](/hybrid-solar-calculator/) will show what size it should be.

A battery left discharged for days also suffers, so let the panels or the grid recharge it fully when they can.

## Check the charging settings

The inverter or PCU must be set for a tubular battery. Settings meant for another battery type can undercharge or overcharge it. If a new battery is fitted, ask whoever installs it to confirm the settings.

## Follow the manual, and keep the warranty card

Each battery comes with the manufacturer's own care instructions and warranty terms. Keep the warranty card and the invoice together. UTL's tubular batteries list warranties from 36 + 24 months, as on the [UIT16036](/products/uit16036-solar-battery/), to 60 months on the [UST1560](/products/utl-150ah-solar-inverter-battery-ust-1560/).

## If you would rather not maintain it

A sealed, maintenance-free battery needs no water at all; UTL describes its [170 Ah USB 17100](/products/utl-170ah-inverter-battery-usb-17100/) that way. A lithium battery needs no water either, and lasts far more cycles; see [how to look after a lithium battery](/lithium-battery-care/) and [tubular or lithium for solar](/blog/lithium-vs-tubular-battery-for-solar/).
