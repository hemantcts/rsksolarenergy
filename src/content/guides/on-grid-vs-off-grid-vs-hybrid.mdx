## On-grid: the one that cuts your bill

An on-grid system feeds your home first and sends any surplus to PSPCL through a bi-directional net meter. At the end of the billing cycle you pay for the net units. There are no batteries, so it is the cheapest system per kW and the only type that gets the PM Surya Ghar subsidy.

The trade-off: it switches off when the grid goes off. That is a safety requirement, not a fault.

In Punjab this is the right choice for most homes above {F.free} units a month and for businesses. Size it to your use. For homes, the goal is usually to bring net units under the {F.free}-unit line, which takes the bill to zero.

## Hybrid: bill savings with backup

A hybrid system is an on-grid system with batteries and a hybrid inverter. It runs the house from solar, charges the batteries, and can use the grid when needed. During a cut, the batteries keep your chosen loads running.

It costs more because of the batteries, which also need replacing during the life of the panels. Choose hybrid when power cuts genuinely cost you something: a home office, a clinic, medical equipment, or a shop that loses sales when the lights go. See [hybrid solar systems](/hybrid-solar-systems/) for the UTL kits we supply.

## Off-grid: no grid connection at all

An off-grid system stores solar power in batteries and runs your loads from them, with no connection to PSPCL. It suits farmhouses, tube-well rooms and sites where the grid is unreliable or not available. Two of our Google reviewers in Mohali chose off-grid systems, a 3 kW and a 5 kW. See [off-grid solar systems](/off-grid-solar-systems/).

## Which one should you choose?

- **Your bill is the problem and you use more than {F.free} units a month:** on-grid.
- **Your bill is the problem and power cuts hurt:** hybrid.
- **There is no reliable grid supply:** off-grid.
- **You use {F.free} units a month or less:** your bill is already zero. Solar makes sense only if backup matters to you, and then it is hybrid or off-grid, paid for without the subsidy.
