## How a hybrid system works

A hybrid system has panels, a hybrid inverter, a battery bank and a connection to PSPCL. During the day, solar runs your home and charges the batteries. When the grid fails, the inverter switches your backed-up circuits to the batteries. When the sun is down and the batteries are low, the grid takes over.

UTL's hybrid inverters let you choose the priority. The Heliac range, for example, can run in a PCU mode (solar, then battery, then grid) or a smart mode (solar, then grid, then battery), so you decide whether the batteries are for daily savings or kept full for cuts.

## When a hybrid system is worth it

- **Power cuts cost you money.** A clinic, a home office, a shop that stops trading, or someone at home who depends on medical equipment.
- **You also want to cut the bill.** A hybrid system still generates your daytime power.
- **You accept a higher price.** Batteries add cost upfront and again when they need replacing, and hybrid systems generally do not get the PM Surya Ghar subsidy.

If power cuts are only an occasional nuisance, an on-grid system with the subsidy plus a small inverter-battery for the essentials is often cheaper. We will tell you which way the numbers fall for your connection.

## Backup is sized to loads, not to the whole house

Batteries that could run air conditioners through a long cut would be very large and expensive. Most hybrid systems back up a chosen set of circuits. Before we quote, we ask what must stay on during a cut and for how long, and size the battery bank to that.
