## How a hybrid system works

<SystemFlow type="hybrid" />

A hybrid system has panels, a hybrid inverter, a battery bank and a connection to PSPCL. During the day, solar runs your home and charges the batteries. If the grid fails, the inverter switches your backed-up circuits over to the batteries, and when the sun is down and the batteries are low, the grid takes over.

UTL's hybrid inverters let you set the priority. The Heliac range, for example, can run in a PCU mode (solar, then battery, then grid) or a smart mode (solar, then grid, then battery), so you decide whether the batteries earn daily savings or stay full for cuts.

## When a hybrid system is worth it

- Power cuts cost you money. Think of a clinic, a home office, a shop that stops trading, or someone at home who relies on medical equipment.
- You want a lower bill as well. A hybrid system still generates your daytime power.
- You are prepared to pay more than for on-grid. Batteries add cost upfront and again when they need replacing. The PM Surya Ghar subsidy still applies, up to {F.cap} for a home, but it does not cover the battery.

If power cuts are only an occasional nuisance, an on-grid system with the subsidy plus a small inverter-battery for the essentials is often cheaper. We will tell you which way the numbers fall for your connection.

## Backup is sized to the loads you choose

Batteries big enough to run air conditioners through a long cut would be very large and expensive, so most hybrid systems back up a chosen set of circuits. Before we quote, we ask what must stay on during a cut and for how long, and size the battery bank to that. You can try it yourself in the [hybrid and off-grid calculator](/hybrid-off-grid-solar-calculator/), which gives the inverter, batteries, panels and roof area for the appliances you pick.
