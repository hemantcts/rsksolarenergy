## 1. Start with the subsidy rules

If you are claiming the PM Surya Ghar subsidy on a home, two rules come first:

- DCR (Domestic Content Requirement): the panels must be made with cells manufactured in India.
- ALMM: the panel model must be on the government's Approved List of Models and Manufacturers.

A panel that is not both loses the subsidy, and it cannot be put right after installation. DCR panels cost more per watt than non-DCR, but on a home system the subsidy usually more than covers the difference. [DCR and non-DCR panels](/dcr-vs-non-dcr-solar-panels/) works through the figures. Without the subsidy, for a business or an off-grid system, non-DCR panels are an option.

## 2. Choose the technology

| | Mono PERC half-cut | TOPCon | Bifacial |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | The established mono cell, cut in half to cut losses | A newer N-type cell design | Collects light on both faces |
| Efficiency | Good | Higher | Depends on the cell |
| Best for | Most rooftops | Limited roof space | Raised structures over a light surface |

Higher efficiency means more watts from the same roof area. On a roof with space to spare, it matters less than price and warranty. See [TOPCon panels](/topcon-solar-panels/), [mono PERC and half-cut](/mono-perc-half-cut-solar-panels/) and [bifacial panels](/bifacial-solar-panels/).

## 3. Read the datasheet

| Datasheet item | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Rated power (Pmax) | The watts at standard test conditions. Decides the panel count. |
| Module efficiency | Power from the area. Higher fits more on the roof. |
| Temperature coefficient of Pmax | How much output falls as the panel heats up. A smaller loss per degree matters in a Punjab summer. |
| Power tolerance | Should be positive only, so no panel is below its rated power. |
| Dimensions and weight | For the layout and the structure. |
| Certifications | The standards the model is tested to, and its ALMM listing. |

[What matters on a panel datasheet](/blog/solar-panel-datasheet-what-matters/) explains each one.

## 4. Check both warranties

Panel warranties come in two parts, both from the manufacturer:

- A product warranty against defects, in years.
- A performance warranty that the panel will still give a stated share of its rated power after a stated number of years.

Get both in writing, with the manufacturer's name. A warranty is only as useful as the claims process behind it, so ask who handles a claim locally.

## 5. Work out how many panels

1. Decide the system size in kW from your bill. The [solar calculator](/solar-calculator/) does this.
2. Divide the size in watts by the panel's wattage, and round up. A 3 kW system with 550 W panels needs six panels.
3. Check the roof: about 100 sq ft of shade-free area per kW, including gaps between rows. The [roof capacity calculator](/tools/solar-roof-capacity-calculator/) counts the panels for you.

## 6. Compare like with like

When quotes differ, check the panels first: make, model, wattage, DCR status and warranties. Two quotes for 3 kW with different panels are different systems. The [quote comparison tool](/tools/solar-quote-comparison/) and the [50-point quote checklist](/solar-guides/solar-quote-checklist/) help.
