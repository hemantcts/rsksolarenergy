## 1. Match the inverter to the system type

| System | Inverter | Battery |
|---|---|---|
| On-grid | Grid-tie inverter | None |
| Hybrid | Hybrid inverter or grid-export PCU with a battery charger | Yes |
| Off-grid | Off-grid solar inverter or PCU | Yes |

An on-grid inverter cannot run batteries. If you think you will want backup later, choose a hybrid inverter now. The [system selector](/tools/solar-system-selector/) helps decide the type.

## 2. Get the size right

- On-grid: match the inverter to the panels. A 3 kW array gets an inverter of about 3 kW.
- Hybrid and off-grid: the inverter's kVA must cover everything that runs at the same time during a cut, plus the brief surge when a fridge, pump, cooler or AC motor starts. Leave headroom; an inverter at its limit every evening trips more often.

The [battery backup calculator](/hybrid-solar-calculator/) sizes the inverter for the loads you choose. For running ACs, see [two 1.5 ton ACs on a 5 kW hybrid](/5kw-hybrid-inverter-two-ac/).

## 3. Match the battery voltage

For hybrid and off-grid systems, the inverter's battery voltage must match the bank: 12 V is one battery, 24 V two, 48 V four and 96 V eight 12 V batteries. Bigger systems use higher voltages to carry the same power at lower current. For a lithium battery, check that the inverter supports lithium charging.

## 4. Single or three phase

Match the inverter to your electricity connection. Most homes are single phase. Larger homes and most commercial buildings are three phase.

## 5. Charge control: MPPT

An MPPT charge controller draws more from the panels than a PWM one, especially when the panel voltage is well above the battery's. On a rooftop system with large panels, choose MPPT. [MPPT and PWM compared](/mppt-vs-pwm-charge-controller/) explains why.

## 6. Transformer or transformerless

Transformerless inverters are lighter, smaller and usually more efficient, and most on-grid and modern hybrid inverters are built this way. Transformer-based units are heavier but rugged, and some off-grid systems rely on their isolation. Whatever the design, check the surge rating against your largest motor. See [transformer and transformerless inverters](/transformer-vs-transformerless-inverter/).

## 7. Monitoring

Choose an inverter that shows what it makes: a display at least, and ideally an app over Wi-Fi or mobile data. Monthly readings are how you notice a fault or dirty panels early. [How to read your solar meter and bill](/read-solar-meter-and-electricity-bill/) shows how to use them.

## 8. Warranty and service

Inverters usually need attention sooner than panels. Before you buy, check:

- the manufacturer's warranty period, in writing;
- what it covers, and whether repair or replacement is on site;
- who handles a claim near you.

[Solar inverter warranties in India](/blog/solar-inverter-warranty-india/) covers what to ask.

## 9. Mounting

Mount the inverter in a shaded, ventilated place out of the rain, close enough to the panels and the meter board to keep cables short. Heat shortens an inverter's life.
