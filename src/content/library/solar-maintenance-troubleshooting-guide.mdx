## Safety first

The panels make power whenever there is light, and the DC side carries high voltage. Never open the inverter, the distribution boxes or a panel's connectors. Never step on panels or climb on the structure. For anything beyond cleaning and reading the display, call your installer.

## The routine

<div class="checklist">

## Every month

1. Note the inverter's total units, and the net meter's import and export, on the same date.
2. Compare this month's units with the same month last year, or with the estimate in your quote.
3. Look at the panels from the roof: dust, bird droppings, leaves, new shade.
4. Look at the inverter's display for warnings.

## When the panels are dirty

5. Clean them early in the morning or in the evening, when they are cool.
6. Use clean water and a soft cloth, sponge or brush on a pole.
7. No pressure washer, detergent or abrasive pad.
8. Clean more often in dry months, near roads and fields, and in the smoke season.

## Every few months

9. Check that cables are still clipped in their conduit or tray and not chafing.
10. Check the structure's fixings and the roof sealing after storms.
11. For tubular batteries: check the water level and top up with distilled water only.
12. Keep the inverter's vents clear of dust and anything stored against it.

## Once a year

13. Have the installer check the earthing and the protection devices.
14. Check that trees or new buildings have not started to shade the panels.
15. Check the warranty cards are still with the invoice.

</div>

## When generation drops: what to check

| What you see | Likely causes | What you can check safely | When to call |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower units than last month | Dust, shorter days, a cloudy or foggy spell | Clean the panels; compare with the same month last year | Panels clean, weather clear, still low |
| Zero units on a sunny day | Grid outage (on-grid switches off), a tripped breaker, an inverter fault | Is the grid on? Is there an error on the display? | Grid is on and the inverter shows an error or nothing |
| Output falls in the afternoon | New shade, or heat | Watch the roof for shade at that time | Shade is not the cause |
| Inverter shows an error | Grid voltage, an earth fault, an internal fault | Photograph the screen | Always; send the photo |
| Battery backup shorter than before | Battery ageing, more load, low water in a tubular battery | What is running in the cut? Tubular water level? | Backup keeps falling |
| Export never rises | You use everything you make, or a meter or wiring issue | Check the net meter's export total on a bright, quiet afternoon | It stays at zero |

Expect seasonal change. We plan on {F.perKwMonth} units a month per kW across the year, with more in spring and early summer and less in foggy December and January. [Generation by location](/tools/solar-generation-punjab/) shows the month-by-month pattern for your town, and [solar output in winter fog](/blog/solar-output-punjab-winter-fog/) explains the winter dip.

## Keep a simple log

A notebook or a phone note with four numbers each month (the inverter total, import, export and the date) shows a problem within weeks instead of at the next bill. [How to read your solar meter and bill](/read-solar-meter-and-electricity-bill/) explains the sum that gives your home's real use.

## Warranty claims

Claims go through the manufacturer's warranty, with the invoice, the warranty card and the serial number. Photograph the fault, note when it started, and send both to your installer to raise the claim.

## Batteries

- Tubular: distilled water only, clean terminals, a cool ventilated place, and not run flat every day. See [tubular battery care](/tubular-battery-care/).
- Lithium: no water; keep it out of the heat and recharge fully when you can. See [lithium battery care](/lithium-battery-care/).
