Net metering is mentioned constantly in solar conversations and rarely explained properly. Here is what actually happens at the meter itself.

## A meter that counts in both directions

A standard electricity meter only counts what you draw from the grid. A net meter is bi-directional: it also counts what your solar system sends back to PSPCL when you generate more than you are using, which for most homes is the middle of a sunny day. Your bill is based on the *net* of these two flows over the billing cycle, not on your gross household consumption.

## Why export is worth less than the units you use

This is the detail that trips up simple solar arithmetic. The rate PSPCL credits for exported surplus is set under PSERC's net metering regulations, and it is not the same as the tariff rate you pay for the units you consume. Export is generally worth less, unit for unit, than self-consumption. This is why sizing a system to cover 100% of your annual use, rather than to bring net use to a sensible target, usually wastes money: the extra capacity mostly produces surplus exported at a lower rate instead of savings at your tariff.

## What this means for sizing

Our approach is to size a system so that most of what it generates is used directly or brought under Punjab's 300-unit free-usage line, rather than maximised for gross export. A bigger system is not automatically a better one once export credit enters the picture.

## Where the net meter fits in the process

The net meter is fitted and inspected by PSPCL as part of installation, and its inspection is the trigger for the commissioning certificate that moves the PM Surya Ghar subsidy process forward. See our [subsidy guide](/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-punjab/) and [what happens on installation day](/blog/solar-installation-day-what-to-expect/) for where this sits in the overall sequence.

## One detail we always confirm

Whether Punjab's 300 free units apply to your gross consumption or your *net* consumption after solar changes the maths for homes near that line. We check this against real, post-installation bills for the connection type before quoting, rather than assuming.
