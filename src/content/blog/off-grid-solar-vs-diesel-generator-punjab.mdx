Solar in Punjab is usually compared with the PSPCL grid. For a real backup need, such as a farmhouse, a tubewell shed, a shop that loses trade in a cut or a clinic that can't lose power mid-procedure, the comparison that matters is off-grid solar against a diesel generator. That is what most people already own, and they are deciding whether to replace it or back it up.

## Running cost is where solar wins

A diesel generator costs less to buy than an equivalent off-grid solar and battery system, kW for kW. It needs diesel every time it runs, though, at a price that tends to rise over the years. Off-grid solar has a real upfront cost and then costs close to nothing to run. There is no fuel to buy or have stolen, and no trip to find diesel during a shortage.

## What a generator does better

- It responds instantly to a big load. A genset can be sized to start an air conditioner or a large motor on demand, while an off-grid battery bank has to be sized for that load in advance, which raises the cost.
- It doesn't depend on the sun. A generator runs as well at 2 a.m. in December as at noon in June, whereas a solar battery bank has to carry you through every hour the panels aren't producing.
- It is quicker to scale up for a while. Renting or buying a bigger generator for a one-off need is simple, while adding solar capacity means a planned installation.

## Noise and exhaust

A generator makes noise and gives off exhaust every single time it runs. Running one near a shop counter, inside a clinic or close to where people sleep is unpleasant on the first day and every day after that. Off-grid solar makes no noise and has no fumes to vent.

## Sizing an off-grid system

An off-grid system is sized to what you actually run and for how long. Lights, fans, a fridge, a computer and a tubewell motor within the inverter's rating are realistic for a well-sized system. A workshop full of heavy machinery, or an air conditioner running for hours, needs a much bigger battery bank, and you may be better off keeping the generator for the heavy or occasional loads while solar covers everyday use.

## Where hybrid changes the calculation

If you also have a working PSPCL connection and only want backup during cuts, a [hybrid system](/hybrid-solar-systems/) usually fits better than pure off-grid. It uses the grid to top up the batteries and still gets the PM Surya Ghar subsidy, which off-grid does not. Off-grid is for sites with no reliable grid to fall back on, such as a remote farmhouse or tubewell shed.
