Most solar comparisons in Punjab are framed as "solar vs the PSPCL grid." For a real backup need, like a farmhouse, a tubewell shed, a shop that loses trade in a cut or a clinic that cannot lose power mid-procedure, the comparison is different: off-grid solar against a diesel generator, because that is what people already own and are deciding whether to replace or supplement.

## Running cost is where solar wins

A diesel generator costs less to buy than an equivalent off-grid solar and battery system, kW for kW. It also needs diesel every time it runs, at a price that only goes one direction over the years. Off-grid solar has a real upfront cost and then close to nothing to run: no fuel, no fuel theft, no trip to buy it during a shortage.

## What a generator does better

- **Instant response to a big load.** A genset can be sized to start an air conditioner or a large motor on demand. An off-grid battery bank has to be sized for that load in advance, which raises the system cost.
- **No dependence on sun hours.** A generator runs at 2 a.m. in December exactly as well as at noon in June. Solar's battery bank has to carry you through the hours the panels are not producing.
- **Faster to scale up temporarily.** Renting or buying a bigger generator for a one-off need is simple. Adding solar capacity is a real, planned installation.

## What a generator does badly, every single time it runs

Noise and exhaust are not occasional problems with a generator. They happen on every run. Running one near a shop counter, inside a clinic, or close to where people sleep is unpleasant on the first day and every day after. Off-grid solar has neither problem: no noise, no fumes, nothing to vent outside.

## The honest sizing question

An off-grid system is sized to what you actually run and for how long, not to "replace the generator entirely" as a slogan. Lights, fans, a fridge, a computer and a tubewell motor within the inverter's rating are realistic for a well-sized off-grid system. A workshop full of heavy machinery, or an air conditioner running for hours, needs a much bigger battery bank and may still be better served by keeping the generator for genuinely heavy or occasional loads while solar covers everyday use.

## Where hybrid changes the calculation

If you also have a working PSPCL connection and just want backup during cuts, a [hybrid system](/hybrid-solar-systems/) is usually the better fit than pure off-grid: it uses the grid to top up the batteries and still gets the PM Surya Ghar subsidy, which pure off-grid does not. Off-grid is for sites with no reliable grid connection to fall back on, such as a remote farmhouse or tubewell shed.
