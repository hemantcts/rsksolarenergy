"What is the price of a solar panel?" is the question we are asked most often on WhatsApp, and it has no single answer. Two panels can both be 540 W and cost quite different amounts. Here is what actually moves the number.

## Price follows watts, then price per watt

Panels are sold by the watt. A 735 W panel costs more than a 165 W one because it makes more power, so the figure to compare is the price per watt, not the price per panel.

Per watt, larger panels are usually the better buy for a rooftop. They also cut everything around them, because a 3 kW array needs far fewer of them:

That is why most home systems we install now use panels in the 500 W to 600 W range. The small ones still have their place on a street light, a pump or a roof with an awkward shape.

## DCR or non-DCR changes the price

DCR panels are made in India with Indian-made cells. Non-DCR panels are not, and they usually cost less per watt.

The choice is made for you if you want the subsidy. PM Surya Ghar only pays on DCR panels from the government's ALMM list, and a panel that is not on the list fails inspection, which cannot be fixed afterwards. Our [subsidy guide](/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-punjab/) covers what gets checked.

Without the subsidy you are free to choose, and a non-DCR system can come in lower than a subsidised one before the subsidy lands. Ask us to price it both ways before deciding.

## The technology in the panel

Our [panels page](/solar-panels/) covers these in detail.

- Mono PERC is the established choice, and the cheapest per watt.
- N-Type TOPCon converts a little more of the same sunlight and holds up better in heat, which matters in a Punjab summer. It costs more.
- Bifacial panels also take light reflected off the roof below them. On a white or light-coloured terrace with an elevated structure, that is a real gain. Flat on a dark roof, it is mostly wasted.

For most homes the gap between these matters less than roof space and price. On a small roof where every square foot counts, the efficient panel earns its premium.

## The panel is not the system

A panel price tells you little about what a system costs. Everything else has to be paid for: the inverter, the mounting structure, DC and AC cable, earthing, the ACDB and DCDB, the lightning arrester, labour, and the net-metering paperwork. The panels are one line in that list.

That is why we publish prices by system size rather than by panel. ## What to ask a supplier

- The exact model and wattage, not "540 watt panel".
- Whether it is DCR and on the ALMM list, if the subsidy matters to you.
- The manufacturer's warranty, product and performance, and who you claim it from.
- The price per watt, so you can compare quotes properly.
- What else is in the quote: structure, cable, earthing and labour, or panels alone.
