For a solar system that uses its batteries every day, lithium. For an inverter that only has to cover the odd power cut, a tubular battery is still the sensible and cheaper choice. What decides it is how often the battery gets discharged.

## When tubular is the right choice

- **Power cuts are occasional.** A battery that sits charged most of the week barely uses its cycle life, so paying extra for more cycles buys you little.
- **Budget matters more than weight.** Tubular is the cheaper way to get a given amount of backup.
- **Your inverter is lead-acid only.** Many home inverters are, and replacing the inverter adds to the cost of switching.
- **There is floor space for it.** A 48 V tubular bank weighs over 200 kg and needs a solid, ventilated spot.

## When lithium pays for itself

- **The battery cycles every day.** Hybrid and off-grid systems often discharge each evening. At one full cycle a day, 1,500 cycles is about {Math.round(cyclesTubular / 365)} years and 3,000 is about {Math.round(cyclesLithium / 365)}.
- **Weight or space is a problem.** One lithium pack is roughly a sixth of the weight of the tubular bank it replaces.
- **Nobody will top up the water.** Lithium needs no maintenance.

## Check these before you switch

- **Lithium support on the inverter.** UTL sells lithium-ready versions, such as the [GAMMA+ 3 kVA, 24 V](/products/gamma-plus-mppt-solar-inverter-3-kva-24-volt-lithium/).
- **The voltage match.** A 25.6 V pack suits a 24 V system; a 48 V or 51.2 V pack suits a 48 V system.
- **One chemistry per bank.** Don't mix lithium and lead-acid batteries on the same inverter.

## What about SMF batteries?

Sealed maintenance-free (SMF) batteries skip the water top-ups, but they are built for shallow, occasional discharge, as in a UPS. For solar that discharges every day, choose tubular or lithium instead.
