## Where the figure comes from

We start from a yield of about {F.yieldRaw} units a year per kW of panels in Punjab, before losses. Taking a fifth off that for real-world losses gives {F.perKwYear} units a year, or {F.perKwMonth} a month. It is a cautious number on purpose: a customer's bill should come in at or below our estimate, not above it.

Output on a real roof falls with:

- **Heat.** Panels lose efficiency as they get hotter, and a Punjab roof in May and June gets very hot.
- **Dust.** A film of dust cuts output noticeably, which is why panels here need regular cleaning.
- **Shade.** Even partial shade from a water tank or a neighbour's wall pulls a whole string of panels down.
- **Direction and tilt.** Panels facing south at a sensible tilt produce the most in Punjab.
- **Wiring and inverter losses.** A small share is always lost between the panels and your meter.

## Summer, monsoon and winter fog

**March to June** brings the longest days and the strongest sun, so generation is at its highest, even though the heat trims some efficiency.

**July to September** is uneven. Monsoon cloud cuts some days sharply, while clear days between the rains still generate well.

**December and January** are the weakest months. The days are short, the sun sits low, and fog and smog cover the plains. A thick foggy morning can produce almost nothing until the fog lifts, and a hazy week lowers output all day. Cold itself isn't the problem; panels work more efficiently when they're cool.

That seasonal swing is why we size home systems on your bills across the year, including your hottest two-month bill, rather than on one good month.

## What this means for sizing

Divide the monthly units you want solar to cover by {F.perKwMonth} to get the kilowatts needed. In Punjab the target is often less than your full use, because bringing net units under {F.free} a month is what takes the bill to zero.
