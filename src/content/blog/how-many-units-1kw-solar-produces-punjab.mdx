## Where the figure comes from

We start from a yield of about {F.yieldRaw} units a year per kW of panels in Punjab, before losses. Taking a fifth off for real-world losses gives {F.perKwYear} units a year, or {F.perKwMonth} a month. We keep it cautious on purpose, so that a customer's bill comes in at or below our estimate.

Several things lower output on a real roof:

- Heat. Panels lose efficiency as they warm up, and a Punjab roof gets very hot in May and June.
- Dust. A film of dust cuts output noticeably, which is why panels here need regular cleaning.
- Shade. Even partial shade from a water tank or a neighbour's wall drags down a whole string of panels.
- Direction and tilt. In Punjab, panels facing south at a sensible tilt produce the most.
- Wiring and inverter losses. A small share is always lost between the panels and your meter.

## Summer, monsoon and winter fog

From March to June the days are longest and the sun strongest, so generation peaks, even though the heat trims some efficiency.

July to September is uneven. Monsoon cloud cuts some days sharply, while clear days between the rains still generate well.

December and January are the weakest months. The days are short, the sun sits low, and fog and smog settle over the plains. A thick foggy morning can produce almost nothing until the fog lifts, and a hazy week lowers output all day. The cold itself isn't the problem, since panels actually work more efficiently when they are cool.

Because of that seasonal swing, we size home systems on your bills across the whole year, including your hottest two-month bill.

## What this means for sizing

Divide the monthly units you want solar to cover by {F.perKwMonth} to get the kilowatts needed. In Punjab the target is often less than your full use, because it is bringing net units under {F.free} a month that takes the bill to zero. [How to size a home system](/blog/how-to-size-home-solar-punjab/) walks through it step by step.
