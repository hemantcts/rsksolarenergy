Most solar maintenance advice is written for other climates. In Punjab, dust and summer heat affect real output more than anything else on a typical checklist, so that is where this guide starts.

## Dust is the biggest factor

Dust on the glass stops sunlight reaching the cells underneath. Punjab's dry season and the farmland around most towns keep it coming, and long dry spells mean it can build up for weeks between showers. In rainier places, the rain does a lot of the cleaning.

Your cheapest maintenance tool is simply looking at the panels. Do it once a month, and clean them when you can see dust building up. A fixed cleaning date may not match your conditions.

## Heat lowers output too

Panels are rated at 25°C, and a rooftop in a Punjab summer runs far hotter than that. Output falls as panel temperature rises. Maintenance can't change this, because it is how the cells behave, but knowing it means a lower reading on a summer afternoon won't be mistaken for a fault.

## A practical checklist

- Look at the panels once a month for dust, bird droppings, fallen leaves or shade from anything that has grown nearby.
- Clean with plain water in the cool part of the day. Early morning is best, and cold water on hot panels can stress the glass over time.
- Check the inverter display or app for error codes, or a daily generation figure that has dropped for no clear reason.
- After storms or strong wind, look over the visible wiring and mounting for damage.
- Compare the net meter reading with what the inverter reports now and then, to catch a metering problem early.

## What maintenance will not fix

Output that falls slowly over months usually points to normal panel degradation, which is gradual and already allowed for in our savings estimates, or to a fault that is developing. Dust is the less likely cause. If cleaning doesn't bring output back to where you expect, have the system checked instead of cleaning it more often.
