Most solar maintenance guides are written for climates that are not Punjab's. Here, dust and summer heat do more to real-world output than anything else on a typical maintenance checklist, and they deserve to be the headline, not a footnote.

## Dust is the main event, not a footnote

A layer of dust across a panel's surface blocks sunlight from reaching the cells underneath, and Punjab's dry season and agricultural surroundings make this a genuine, ongoing factor rather than an occasional inconvenience. Unlike many climates where rain does most of the cleaning for you, long dry spells here mean dust can sit and accumulate for weeks between rains.

**What to actually do:** a visual check is the cheapest maintenance tool you have. Look at the panels monthly, and clean when dust is visibly building up rather than waiting for a fixed calendar date that may not match your actual conditions.

## Heat affects performance, not just comfort

Panels are rated at a standard 25°C; a rooftop in a Punjab summer regularly runs far hotter than that, and output drops as panel temperature rises. There is no maintenance fix for this — it is physics, not a fault — but it is worth knowing so a lower summer-afternoon output reading is not mistaken for a system problem.

## A practical checklist

- **Visual check, monthly.** Look for dust buildup, bird droppings, leaf litter or shading from new growth nearby.
- **Clean with plain water,** in the cool part of the day — early morning is ideal. Avoid cold water on hot panels; the temperature shock can stress the glass over time.
- **Check the inverter display or app** for error codes or an unexplained drop in daily generation.
- **Inspect visible wiring and mounting** for obvious damage after storms or heavy wind.
- **Keep an eye on the net meter reading** against what the inverter reports, to catch a metering issue early.

## What maintenance will not fix

A steadily declining output over months, rather than a sudden drop, is more likely panel degradation (normal, slow, and already accounted for in our savings estimates) or a developing fault than a cleaning problem. If cleaning does not restore expected output, that is the point to get the system properly checked rather than keep cleaning more often.
