If your rooftop solar is exporting a lot but your bill has not dropped as you hoped, the fix is usually to shift more of your use into the sunlit hours. Under net metering, the units you use as they are made are worth the most because they replace units you would have bought.

<Callout kind="problem">

“My net meter shows big exports at noon, but my bill is still high. Did I buy the wrong size, or is my system underperforming?”

</Callout>

## What is causing the mismatch

- Self use vs export. Midday when your array is strongest, many homes are empty. The power goes out to the grid as export. In the evening, you import again.  
- Sizing without the daytime pattern. If you sized only from the total monthly units on the bill, you may have more kW than your daytime loads can actually consume.  
- Winter and weather. Output is an estimate that depends on your roof, shade, dust and weather. In December and January fog, plan on about 75% of the average. A cloudy week, dust or shade will also cut output.  
- The 300 free units line. If your two-month bill often sits under 600 units, there may be little to save unless you cross that line in the hotter months.

To see what is really happening, read your meter’s import and export the right way. We walk through that step by step in our guide: [how to read your solar meter and bill](/read-solar-meter-and-electricity-bill/).

## What to do this month

<Callout kind="fix">

- Put the heaviest appliances you can into daytime: water heater, washing machine, dishwasher, ironing, EV charging.
- Use timers on geysers and washing machines so they start between 11 am and 3 pm.
- If you work from home, charge laptops and phones through the day, not overnight.
- Check shade and dust. Clean if needed. If a tree or tank shades the array, we can look at a layout change.
- If you are still planning a system, size from your daytime use with [the calculator](/solar-calculator/), not just the total on the bill.

</Callout>

Rooftop output in Punjab averages about 135 units a month from each kW across the year. With TOPCon panels, output can reach up to about 180 units per kW in the sunniest months. These are estimates that depend on your roof, shade, dust and weather. Use them to plan, then match your use to the hours your system makes those units.

You also need the space. Plan about 100 sq ft of shade-free roof per kW. Your rooftop system can be at most 100 percent of your sanctioned load.

## Choose the system type with this in mind

On-grid and hybrid systems qualify for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy. Off-grid does not. Panels must be DCR and on the ALMM list. If you are deciding between types, start with how often you face power cuts and how you will use power in the day.

| Option | Works during power cuts | Qualifies for subsidy | Who it suits for daytime use |
|---|---|---|---|
| On-grid | No | Yes | Homes that can run big loads in the day and do not need backup |
| Hybrid (with battery) | Yes (on the backed-up circuits) | Yes | Homes that also want backup and can still run daytime loads directly from solar |
| Off-grid | Yes (array and battery sized for it) | No | Sites without a grid connection or where net metering is not planned |

Read the pros and cons in more detail here: [on-grid vs off-grid vs hybrid](/on-grid-vs-off-grid-vs-hybrid/). If backup is the main goal, plan the battery properly. Lithium is planned at 80 percent usable each cycle, tubular at 50 percent. Our [hybrid systems overview](/hybrid-solar-systems/) and [lithium battery care](/lithium-battery-care/) explain what to expect.

## A simple change that shifts the numbers

<Callout kind="insight">

Under net metering, your exports are a by-product, not the target. The units you use as they are made are the ones worth most, so the lever you control is when you use power. A timer on a geyser or washer at noon can move more real rupees than a new panel would, because it turns exported units into used units. That is why we size from daytime use first, and give export no value at all in our calculations.

</Callout>

Here is a practical way to start:
- Put two appliances on timers for the next four weeks, between 11 am and 3 pm.  
- Read and note your meter’s import and export once a week. Use our [meter and bill guide](/read-solar-meter-and-electricity-bill/) to be sure you are reading the right registers.  
- If import drops and export steadies, keep those timers. If midday export still spikes, list one more daytime load to shift.
