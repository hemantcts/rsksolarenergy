A 25-year panel warranty is the headline figure in most solar conversations. The inverter, the part more likely to need attention over the system's life, usually carries a much shorter one, and that gap is worth understanding before you buy, not after something fails.

## Two different lifespans, by design

A solar panel is largely passive: silicon cells behind glass, with no moving parts and nothing to wear out mechanically. An inverter is active electronics, with switching components, capacitors and often a cooling fan, running whenever the sun is up. Electronics age faster than glass and silicon. A typical inverter or PCU warranty in India runs 3 to 5 years, against a panel's 25-year performance warranty. This is an industry-wide pattern, not a shortfall specific to one brand.

## What the warranty actually covers

A standard inverter warranty covers manufacturing defects: a fault in the unit as built, not damage from how it was used. It generally does not cover:

- **Surge and lightning damage.** A grid surge or lightning strike is typically excluded unless a fitted surge protection device failed to do its job.
- **Incorrect installation.** Wiring outside spec, wrong voltage, or installation by someone not authorised to do it.
- **Physical damage.** Water ingress, impact damage, rodent damage to cabling.
- **Normal wear items.** Cooling fans in particular are a common wear item across the industry.

## Why this matters for budgeting, not just for peace of mind

If an inverter typically needs replacing or servicing well before the panels do, that is a real cost to plan for over a system's life, not a surprise a few years in. When we quote a system, we tell you the inverter's warranty term plainly alongside the panel's, rather than letting a single "25-year warranty" headline imply everything is covered for that long.

## What to check before you buy

- The exact warranty period on the inverter model quoted, not the panel's
- Whether a surge protection device is included, and whether it is itself covered
- Who provides service during the warranty: the installer, or the manufacturer directly
- What documentation you need to keep for a claim
