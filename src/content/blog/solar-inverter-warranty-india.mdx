Most solar conversations lead with the 25-year panel warranty. The inverter is the part more likely to need attention over the life of the system, and its warranty is usually much shorter. It is worth knowing that before you buy.

## Why panels and inverters last differently

A solar panel is largely passive: silicon cells behind glass, with no moving parts to wear out. An inverter is active electronics. It has switching components, capacitors and often a cooling fan, and it works whenever the sun is up. Electronics age faster than glass and silicon, so a typical inverter or PCU warranty in India runs 3 to 5 years, against a 25-year performance warranty on the panels. You will see the same gap across brands.

## What the warranty actually covers

A standard inverter warranty covers manufacturing defects, meaning faults in the unit as it was built. Damage caused by how the unit was used or installed is treated differently, and warranties generally exclude:

- Surge and lightning damage, unless a fitted surge protection device failed to do its job.
- Incorrect installation, such as wiring outside spec, the wrong voltage, or an installer who was not authorised.
- Physical damage from water getting in, knocks, or rodents chewing through cabling.
- Parts that wear out in normal use. Cooling fans are the usual example.

## Plan for it in your budget

If the inverter is likely to need servicing or replacing well before the panels, that is a cost to plan for over the life of the system. When we quote, we put the inverter's warranty term next to the panel's, so a single "25-year warranty" line does not suggest the whole system is covered for that long.

## What to check before you buy

- The warranty period on the exact inverter model you are quoted, which is separate from the panel's
- Whether a surge protection device is included, and whether it has its own cover
- Who handles service during the warranty: the installer or the manufacturer
- Which documents you need to keep for a claim
