Every solar quote leads with a wattage number: 400 W, 540 W, and so on. It is the easiest figure to compare and the least useful one on its own, especially for a Punjab roof where summer heat is the real test.

## Module efficiency: the same roof area, more or less power

Module efficiency tells you how much of the sunlight hitting the panel becomes electricity. A higher-efficiency panel produces more power from the same roof area, which matters if roof space is your constraint rather than budget. Two panels with the same wattage rating can have different efficiencies if they are physically different sizes; check the dimensions alongside the wattage.

## Temperature coefficient: the number that matters most in a Punjab summer

Panels are rated at a standard test condition of 25°C. A rooftop in Punjab in June regularly runs panels far hotter than that, and output drops as panel temperature rises. The rate of that drop is the temperature coefficient, usually shown as a percentage loss per degree above 25°C. A panel with a better (smaller) coefficient loses less output on the hottest days of the year, which is when a household's air-conditioning load is highest.

## ALMM listing: not a technical spec, but it decides your subsidy

The Approved List of Models and Manufacturers is a government list of panel models eligible for PM Surya Ghar. It is not a quality signal in the technical sense; it is a compliance requirement. A panel that performs identically to a listed model but is not itself on the list still fails inspection and forfeits the subsidy, with no way to fix it after the system is installed. Always check the *exact model number* on your quote against the current ALMM list before you commit, not just the brand name. For a subsidised home system the panel also has to be DCR, meaning built with Indian-made cells.

## Two warranties, not one

Panel warranties come in two parts that get conflated in sales conversations:

- **Product warranty:** covers manufacturing defects in the panel itself, typically a shorter period.
- **Performance warranty:** guarantees the panel still produces a stated share of its original rated output after a long period, commonly quoted around 25 years, often with a steeper allowed drop in the first year and a slower decline after.

"25-year warranty" as a single unqualified line in a quote should prompt the question: 25 years on what, exactly: the panel not failing, or the panel still producing power at a guaranteed level?
