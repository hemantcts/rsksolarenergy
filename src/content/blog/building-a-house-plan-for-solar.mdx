A house takes months to build and a solar system takes days to install, so there is no need to do them together. There is a lot you can do while the house is going up that makes the solar simple, and cheap, afterwards.

Most of it costs nothing at this stage. All of it is awkward once the plaster is on.

## Eight things to do while you build

### 1. Ask PSPCL for enough sanctioned load

A rooftop system can be at most {SOLAR_CONFIG.netMetering.maxSystemPercentOfSanctionedLoad}% of your sanctioned load. Apply for the new connection with the system in mind: 5 kW of load for a 5 kW system. Going back later for load enhancement is a separate application, and it has to be done before the solar one.

### 2. Keep the south side of the roof clear

Panels need about {SOLAR_CONFIG.generation.sqFtPerKw} sq ft each per kW, and they need it without shade. Put the water tanks, the stair cabin and the dish on the north side, where their shadows fall away from the panels. Winter shadows are much longer than the ones you see in June.

### 3. Plan the structure while the slab is designed

Panels usually sit on an elevated structure, which keeps the terrace usable underneath. Tell whoever designs the slab that it will carry one, and finish the waterproofing before anything is fixed to it.

### 4. Leave a conduit from the roof to the inverter

A 40 mm conduit from the terrace down to where the inverter will hang saves chasing finished walls. Put the inverter somewhere shaded and ventilated, close to the main distribution board, not in a sealed cupboard and not in the afternoon sun.

### 5. Put in an earthing pit for the solar

Solar needs its own earthing, plus a lightning arrester on the roof. Digging a pit costs little while the site is already dug up and a great deal once the driveway is laid.

### 6. Decide about backup before the wiring is done

If you want lights and fans to run through a power cut, they need to sit on their own backup board. Doing that now costs a few hundred rupees in wire. Doing it later means rewiring circuits. The [hybrid calculator](/hybrid-solar-calculator/) shows what a battery for those circuits looks like.

### 7. Put a water tap on the terrace

Panels need a wash every few weeks in dusty and smoky months. A tap on the roof turns that into a five-minute job. Carrying buckets upstairs is how panels end up never being cleaned.

### 8. Keep the connection and the paperwork in the owner's name

The subsidy is paid to the person who owns the house and holds the electricity connection, into a bank account in the same name. Mismatched names are one of the most common reasons applications stall. The [subsidy guide](/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-punjab/) has the full list of documents.

## What the order of events looks like

1. Sanctioned load applied for with the solar system in mind.
2. House finished, PSPCL connection live in the owner's name.
3. Application on the PM Surya Ghar portal, and PSPCL approves feasibility.
4. Installation, which takes a few days for a home system.
5. PSPCL fits the net meter and inspects, then the subsidy reaches your bank account.

Nothing should be installed before step 3, which is why the panels wait until the house has its own connection.
