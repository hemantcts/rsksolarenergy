The society subsidy is ₹18,000 per kW up to 500 kW, and it is paid into the applicant’s own bank account 30 to 45 days after the DISCOM inspection.

## Who signs, and what name goes on the application

If you need the step-by-step on forms and uploads, use our guide for societies here: [Rooftop solar for housing societies and RWAs in Punjab](/housing-society-solar/).

## How the society subsidy works

For housing societies, the PM Surya Ghar subsidy amount is ₹18,000 per kW up to 500 kW. It is released 30 to 45 days after the DISCOM inspection, directly to the applicant’s bank account. It does not reduce the upfront invoice amount, and it is not payable to any installer or financier.

On-grid and hybrid systems qualify. Off-grid does not. Panels must be domestic content requirement compliant and on the ALMM list. If you want the full slab details and the steps, see [PM Surya Ghar Subsidy in Punjab 2026](/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-punjab/).

## What size a society can install

Two simple caps decide the size:

- Sanctioned load on the common-area meter: a rooftop system can be at most 100% of that sanctioned load.
- Usable roof: plan about 100 sq ft of shade-free roof per kW.

We will verify both against your PSPCL bill and rooftop photos. For meter types and system architectures, see [On-grid vs Off-grid vs Hybrid in Punjab](/on-grid-vs-off-grid-vs-hybrid/).

## What to expect from generation

As a planning figure in Punjab, we use about 135 units a month from each kW of panels, averaged across the year. In the sunniest months, TOPCon panels can reach up to about 180 units per kW. In December and January fog, plan on about 75% of the average. These are estimates that depend on your roof, shade, dust and weather.

- The units your lifts, pumps, lighting and STP use as they are made are worth most, because they replace units that would have been bought.
- Whatever is exported is settled under PSPCL’s net metering rules. In our calculations, we give export no value, so no saving figure of ours depends on it. For how credits are shown on the bill, see [Net metering in Punjab](/blog/net-metering-punjab-pspcl-explained/).

## What qualifies for the subsidy: panels and system type

- System type: on-grid and hybrid qualify. Hybrid costs more than on-grid at the same size because it includes a battery and a hybrid inverter. Off-grid does not qualify and is priced on a different basis. If backup is required for lifts, pumps and lighting, read our notes on [hybrid systems](/hybrid-solar-systems/).
- Panels: they must be DCR and on the ALMM list. Our explainer on what those lists mean, and how they affect supply and price, is here: [DCR vs non-DCR panels](/dcr-vs-non-dcr-solar-panels/).

If you are comparing panel technologies for limited roof area, see [TOPCon panels](/topcon-solar-panels/) and [Which panel technology is best](/best-solar-panel-technology/).

## Practical checks we do before quoting

- PSPCL bill for the common-area connection: to confirm sanctioned load and consumer name.
- Rooftop photos and a simple sketch: to confirm area, shade and access.
- Essential loads list: lifts, fire pumps, STP, lighting circuits. If backup is needed, we plan battery usable energy at 80% for lithium and 50% for tubular in each cycle, and we will map what must run during an outage.

Our smallest installed sizes are 3 kW on-grid and 1 kW hybrid or off-grid. For larger common roofs, we design in blocks so maintenance and cleaning are easy.
